BRK - Relay

User manual and parameter reference · 0.1.0

A sixteen-step router with per-lane Motion modulation, four delay/filter/phaser lanes, beat/ms/pitched delay modes, Flutter, per-send pan and volume, and an original-sound row.

Quick startOperationEvery parameterPresets and filesInstallation and help

Quick start

  1. Init passes original sound at full volume, with IN locked, neutral effect pads and no effect sends. Start the host transport and use HOST SPEED as a tempo multiplier, or turn HOST off for internal TEMPO from 20 to 999 BPM.
  2. Click cells to toggle sends; drag X for pan and Y for level. The bottom IN row independently sets original-sound volume; zero is mute and other sends stay unchanged.
  3. Shape delay/feedback, filter position/width, phaser speed/mix and Flutter speed/depth (both speeds up to 30 Hz) with the four pads on each lane.

Editor window

1360 by 524 pixels, with all sixteen steps on one page and an optional contextual HELP footer.

Operation

Each lane has a sixteen-step Motion strip and an M mouse-down toggle that opens to the right for eight simultaneous signed target amounts, STEP/GLIDE and random limits. Dice, lock and reset sit visibly beside each send and Motion row; effect lane resets sit on the right. Releasing M keeps the panel open; another M mouse-down, outside mouse-down or Escape closes it. Motion continues through empty sends; it can offset time, filter position/width, phaser speed/mix, Flutter speed/depth and wet-return pan together. Each amount is independent, from -100% to +100%; zero disables that target. Its ranges start fully open, and Init starts with all amounts at zero. Sequencer dice also rolls matching Motion curves, respecting independent sequence/Motion locks and preserving all target amounts. The Motion dice changes only its curve; effect dice preserves it. Clocked routing lands exactly on the sample boundary, avoiding the outgoing step capturing a new transient. All four tails continue after the step changes, retaining the pan of their input. MIX blends original input with the complete sequenced output. The sequencer reset icon clears sends and pan on unlocked rows and restores unlocked IN to 100%; row locks protect their contents. Effect lane resets restore only unlocked pairs in that lane. The header FX reset also resets shared controls in unprotected columns; locks are respected. Sequence and Motion configuration stay stored. Init uses those same positions: centered delay, full-width open filter, bottom-left phaser and Flutter.

TIME cycles SYNC, MS and PITCH on one shared axis from two samples to a 250 ms base maximum at 100% Time Scale. SYNC chooses beat fractions inside that range; MS is continuous; PITCH chooses chromatic note periods and displays intervals relative to ROOT (default C3). Switching modes changes only the snap. AXIS offers LIN (Init default, about 125 ms at the center) or LOG (finer short-delay control); older sounds retain LOG. CHANGE chooses a 20 ms FADE between stationary delay positions or GLIDE with audible pitch bends. TIME SCALE reaches 1000%; it and ROOT extend the shared target before snapping, up to one bar at the active tempo and meter, with the same physical limits in every mode. A: FB uses constant feedback up to 99.9%, displayed without rounding to 100%, with 75% at the pad midpoint. It has no step envelope or feedback reset. The fully open BAND filter passes the loop unchanged even with resonance raised. Active filtering and Flutter’s fractional interpolation inside the loop can still reduce repeat energy; only B applies the envelope. B: ENV uses a unity feedback peak with a per-lane decay envelope over two sequencer items, continuing through an empty next item and retriggering on new sends. Y shapes short, linear or held decay; the curve and live marker appear inside each pad. Delay time appears inside the box. A/B and the time selector sit beside DELAY. FILTER adds BAND/NOTCH and R resonance; PHASER adds R resonance and S stereo spread; FLUTTER adds SINE/DRIFT and S spread. These header controls affect the whole column, and mono ignores stereo spread. R means resonance; S means stereo modulation spread. Filter resonance peaks, phaser left/right response curves and real SINE/DRIFT Flutter paths visualize the settings. R/S value boxes match toggle height. Padlocks protect both axes of each effect pair.

Each sequencer row, including IN, has its dice, lock and reset on the left; the lock protects the row from randomization. There is no global sequence lock or sequencer column dice; the sequencer reset icon preserves locked rows. Each active send has a centered pan rail and a volume stem aligned with its XY dot.

Delay timing and effect lanes

The selector beside the DELAY title cycles SYNC → MS → PITCH. All three modes use the same physical delay axis, from two samples to a base maximum of 250 ms at 100% Time Scale. They share the same X position and random range; changing mode changes the snap only. Y controls conventional feedback in A or the decay curve in B. Time Scale can extend the upper limit, capped at one bar of the active tempo and meter. The lower end is sample-aware, so it remains useful all the way to two samples.

  • SYNC: snap to the closest beat fraction inside the shared time range. Straight, triplet and dotted values are available through 1/2048, with finer binary divisions down to the two-sample limit. Rational fractions such as 3/128, 5/128 and 7/128 are included where they fit the range at the current tempo. Equivalent timings appear once: 6/128 = 3/64 = 1/32 dotted. At 120 BPM and 100% scale, the 250 ms end corresponds to 1/8. Changing tempo updates the grid around the same physical time target.
  • MS: continuous time from two samples to 250 ms, independent of tempo until the one-bar limit is reached. Two samples are approximately 0.0417 ms at 48 kHz or 0.0208 ms at 96 kHz.
  • PITCH: snap to the closest chromatic note period inside the same range. ROOT is C2–C4, default C3; each lane shows its relative semitone interval and resulting note. Intervals extend in both directions as needed to cover the common band. ROOT transposes the shared delay target, so the changed timing is retained when leaving PITCH. There is no scale quantizer.

AXIS selects LIN or LOG for all three snap modes. Init uses LIN: equal horizontal distances give equal changes in physical time, so the center is about 125 ms at 100% scale and the default root. LOG keeps detailed control of the shortest delays; its center is about 2.2 ms. Switching SYNC/MS/PITCH retains the same X coordinate and axis. Older saved sounds keep LOG. TIME SCALE is edited in its value box; the redundant rotary knob is removed. RANGES leads the top control row.

TIME SCALE adjusts the shared target from 25 to 1000% before snapping. In PITCH the value box shows ROOT instead; both settings remain in effect across all modes. At the default root, 200% extends the maximum to 500 ms and 400% to one second; 1000% requests 2.5 seconds, capped at one bar (2 seconds in 4/4 at 120 BPM). ROOT transposition also affects the maximum. All modes snap within the same scaled bounds. At the very shortest endpoint the readout shows two samples, which can fall between notes or beat fractions. Other snapped readouts show the exact division or note actually used. Flutter is bounded by the same limits.

CHANGE selects FADE or GLIDE. FADE crossfades old and new stationary delay taps over 20 ms. GLIDE continuously moves a delay read with a 20 ms smoothing time, creating pitch bends during time changes. Flutter remains a continuous read modulation in both settings.

Each delay box shows its effective time inside the pad, with the beat division or root-relative note and Y value below. At 120 BPM, host speed ×1 and time scale 100%:

Double-click the TIME SCALE or ROOT value box for precise numeric entry. In A, Y on every delay pad sets feedback from 0 to 99.9%. The curve rises faster than a linear control: the midpoint is 75%, leaving the upper half for the long-decay region. Feedback range rectangles use the same curve. Values near the maximum show one decimal, so 99.9% is displayed as 99.9%, rather than 100%. Percentage feedback does not use the envelope or reset between sequencer steps; only new input routing changes at step boundaries. The envelope acts only in B mode.

Use A: FB / B: ENV beside the delay selector to compare the two behaviors. A retains conventional feedback. B starts each active lane’s sequencer item with a 100% feedback peak and applies a decay envelope over two items, following STEP and the current tempo. Bottom Y is a very short curve, center is linear and top holds near full feedback before falling. Consecutive active items retrigger the envelope. An empty following item lets the existing envelope continue through its second half; it reaches zero after two items. Audio-block boundaries do not retrigger it. The filled curve and live marker appear inside the XY box. A and B retain separate Y values and random ranges while sharing delay X. Time Scale changes delay time, while envelope length remains two sequencer items.

  • Filter: the header selects BAND or NOTCH. BAND passes the selected band; NOTCH removes that band using low/high outside bands. R sets resonance for the entire column, from 0 to 100%. Zero R with BAND preserves the existing filter. Resonant filtering uses the shared state-variable filters, with loop gain compensation so resonance boosts do not multiply indefinitely on every repeat. X moves the band on a logarithmic frequency axis; Y changes its width. Low-cut and high-cut edges are shown. Each edge uses two cascaded first-order stages, 12 dB/octave, with cutoffs ordered and limited below Nyquist. At full width the readout is OPEN and the filter passes the delayed signal unchanged, including frequencies outside the nominal display band. The R/S header value boxes match the adjacent toggles in height. R is resonance, S is stereo modulation spread. Filter pads show their frequency response and resonance peaks; phaser pads show resonance-shaped notches plus left/right response and modulation paths. Flutter pads show the actual SINE or smooth random DRIFT function used by the audio engine. Spread adds a second path, following the left/right phase offset. The modulation phases animate during processing. A fully open filter or a phaser at zero mix stays transparent; narrow the filter or raise phaser mix to hear resonance.

  • Phaser: R adds normalized feedback inside the phaser for stronger resonance; S offsets left/right modulation phase for stereo spread. Both header values apply to all lanes, 0–100%, with neutral defaults. X sets speed from 0.03 to 30 Hz; Y sets the mix of the six-stage all-pass phaser. The phaser processes the wet return outside the feedback loop.

  • Flutter: choose SINE for periodic modulation or DRIFT for smooth, repeatable random movement. S offsets left/right modulation phase from 0 to 100%; mono ignores stereo spread. The waveform and spread apply to all lanes. X sets speed from 0.1 to 30 Hz; Y sets depth from 0 to 6 ms. It modulates the delay read position for pitch movement on the repeats in all three time modes. Zero depth is neutral at every speed. Depth and speed changes use short ramps; modulation remains causal and within the delay cap.

  • Padlocks: above each effect pad. Closed, filled locks protect both axes of that pair during all-lane, individual-lane and effect-column dice randomization. Open, outlined locks allow randomization and reset. Locks also protect FX resets; manual edits and host automation remain available.

Grab an effect dot to drag without a pickup jump, or click elsewhere in its pad to place it at the cursor. Filter dragging follows the displayed band center as width changes. Shift gives fine movement without jumping. Dragging an empty sequencer cell starts at the cursor position rather than full level. Double-click an effect pad to open its two precise value cells. The shared delay-position field uses a percentage; the pad readout shows its effective time and snap. ROOT accepts sharp or flat note names such as C#3 and Db3. Each supports dragging, scrolling and double-click numeric entry through the shared controls. On effect pads, scroll adjusts X and Alt-scroll adjusts Y. Tab reaches a send cell; Return toggles it, left/right adjusts pan and up/down adjusts level. On the IN row, arrows adjust original level. On effect pads, left/right adjusts X and up/down adjusts Y; Return opens precise values. Space remains available to the host.

Motion

To hear a first example, narrow a lane’s filter, click M, raise FILTER POS and roll the Motion dice beside that row. Send audio to the lane or let an existing tail ring.

Every effect lane has its own sixteen signed Motion values, aligned with the routing steps. M toggles a panel to its right on mouse-down, exposing eight independent signed amounts: TIME, FILTER POS, FILTER WIDTH, PHASER SPEED, PHASER MIX, FLUTTER SPEED, FLUTTER DEPTH and PAN. Several targets can move together from one curve. Each amount runs from −100% to +100%; negative amounts reverse movement, and zero disables that target. The curve offsets each pad’s stored value. PAN moves the wet return around center, independently of the send’s original pan. Motion continues through steps with no send. Init starts with all eight amounts at zero.

STEP holds a value until the next item, with a short anti-click ramp. GLIDE joins it to the following value across the whole item, including the wrap from step 16 to step 1. Timing follows the same host/internal sequencer clock. Delay Motion follows LIN/LOG and SYNC/MS/PITCH snapping. CHANGE controls whether that changing delay fades or bends in pitch. Amount changes use short ramps.

Drag each Motion point vertically; Shift gives fine movement without a pickup jump. Scroll or keyboard arrows adjust it; Return centers it, and double-click opens a precise value field. The same panel contains a larger curve editor, the eight amounts, STEP/GLIDE and random minimum/maximum. It stays to the right for every row, shifting vertically to fit. Releasing M leaves it open; the next mouse-down on M closes it. Outside mouse-down and Escape also dismiss it. The limits start fully open at −100% to +100%.

Each Motion row has its dice, lock and reset visible on the left, in the same order as the send row above it. Its dice changes only that curve. Sequencer dice also rolls the corresponding Motion curves, preserving all eight amounts; the sequence and Motion locks protect their own rows independently. The Motion reset zeroes its sixteen values and retains the amounts, shape and ranges. Sequence/effect resets preserve Motion. Thin live pad markers show the combined offsets while the main dot stays at the stored position; the delay readout follows the modulated time.

Older single-destination Motion patches migrate their existing amount to the matching dial, leaving the other seven amounts at zero. Their curves, shape, limits and lock remain stored.

Randomization and reset

Init sets every random minimum and maximum to the full available range, for all lane effects (including both A/B controls), each sequencer lane and IN volume. Saved presets and sessions keep their stored limits.

The lane dice changes delay positions and other lane effects only, using their individual ranges and padlocks. The dice at the right end of the effect title row randomizes all unlocked lane effects; each lane’s dice on the far right changes that lane only. A padlock beside the dice at the right of each effect lane protects the entire lane from every effect randomizer. A dice beside each DELAY, FILTER, PHASER and FLUTTER title changes that effect column across all lanes, respecting both lane and individual effect locks. Delay-column dice changes the shared time and only the active A/B Y control; the other mode’s Y value stays stored. RANGES shows a shaded minimum/maximum rectangle on all sixteen effect pads and each of the four sequencer rows at once. The IN row has its own volume-only range. On the sequencer, X sets pan bounds and Y sets level bounds; each row has independent limits. Drag an edge or corner to resize, drag inside to move the complete area or drag outside to draw a fresh area. Shift gives fine movement. Double-click or Return opens precise bounds and an Apply lane ranges to all lanes button. Arrows move the area; DONE returns to sound editing.

The sequence dice changes sends, pan, independent IN volumes and the matching Motion curves, leaving effect pads, target amounts and the clock unchanged. Each lock at the left of a sequencer row protects that row across all sixteen steps, including a separate lock for IN. The dice at the left of a send row, followed by its lock and reset, randomizes that row and its corresponding Motion curve. Their locks apply independently. IN has no Motion curve. There is no global sequencer lock or per-step/column dice. IN starts locked, so sequence randomization preserves its full level until you unlock it. Sequence locks protect both randomization and sequence reset. RANGES, DENSITY and MAX LANES lead the top row before RUN. The header and row resets use circular return-arrow icons with tooltips. Each section’s reset and global dice share its title row, removing the separate toolbar row. The selected-step numeric footer has been removed.

Density is tested independently for effect sends and IN on each step. Every step selected for effects chooses between one and the configured maximum number of distinct lanes. Each effect lane uses its own pan and level range, and IN uses its own level range. When randomizing one row, existing sends in other rows also count toward MAX LANES, so the row cannot add a send beyond that limit. Locked active lanes count toward MAX LANES; they remain unchanged even if already above the new maximum. Unselected unlocked values are zero and unused unlocked sends are centered. Each sequencer row’s reset clears sends and centers pan only in that unlocked row, or restores unlocked IN to 100%. The header sequencer reset icon clears sends and centers pan on unlocked lanes, and restores unlocked IN values to 100%. Locked rows stay unchanged. Lane effects, clock settings, locks and random ranges are retained.

Each effect lane has dice, lock and reset on the right. Its reset restores only that lane’s unlocked effect pairs, preserving shared header settings and every other lane. The header effect reset restores unlocked effect pads without changing the sequence, Motion amounts/curves, clock, A/B selection or ranges. Lane and pair locks protect both randomization and reset. Shared header controls reset only when their column has no locked lane or pair. Delay X goes to the center; A feedback goes to its visual midpoint (75%) and B’s curve to linear. Filter goes to top-center and OPEN. Phaser and Flutter go to bottom-left: minimum speed, zero mix/depth. These are also Init’s effect positions. Existing delay contents continue through the normal time/control transitions.

Clock and audio

HOST reads tempo, meter and PPQ each block, applying HOST SPEED to tempo and PPQ together. MS and PITCH use this tempo only for the one-bar ceiling; the sequencer still follows the clock. Routing boundaries are evaluated per sample, including negative PPQ and transport jumps. Without a host position, the internal clock supplies the fallback, using HOST SPEED while HOST remains on. HOST off ignores the DAW clock and host multiplier. Source tempo bounds are 20–999 BPM, giving 5–3996 effective BPM in host mode; meters support up to sixteen quarter-note beats per bar.

Clocked send, pan and IN changes land exactly on the sequencer sample boundary. This prevents an outgoing step from capturing the next step’s transient, including step 16 into step 1. Manual edits within an item and effect controls use short ramps. Delay changes use the selected FADE/GLIDE behavior; Flutter moves its read position continuously with interpolation. The filter and the Flutter-modulated delay read sit inside the feedback loop, while the phaser sits outside it. A fully open BAND filter is a true unity bypass even when its resonance control is raised. Active filtering, its resonance gain compensation, and fractional read interpolation can still reduce loop energy. Flutter continuously moves the read fraction, which can darken and shorten repeats without any feedback envelope. Feedback percentage applies on every trip through the loop: at 99.9%, an ideal two-sample loop at 48 kHz falls 60 dB in about 0.29 seconds; a 15.625 ms loop takes about 108 seconds. Short delay times therefore need gains much closer to unity for equally long tails.

Mono and stereo are supported. Delay storage is allocated during prepare and released in releaseResources, never resized on the audio thread. Storage reserves five seconds per lane for the maximum scale and root shift. Four stereo lanes use approximately 7.32 MiB of delay storage at 48 kHz, doubling at 96 kHz. No audio data or external samples are stored in presets.

Effect tails are reported to the host for offline rendering. This development build still needs listening in the target DAW, including tempo/meter changes, Flutter, pan automation and project recall.

Presets and sessions

The shared preset bar saves .relay files. Sound presets retain MIX, A/B mode, both sets of Y controls, the sequence, original levels, pan, lanes, clock controls, padlocks and random ranges, excluding RUN and BYPASS. Host sessions retain every parameter. Session recall does not dirty the host project. There are no embedded factory presets yet; Init supplies full original volume with no effect sequence.

Older development presets and sessions retain sends to lanes 1–4. Sends to removed lanes 5–8 become no send. Old dry values are discarded. Stored IN levels are retained. Old musical time controls first migrate into the former normalized position by their fractional ratio. Existing delay positions and range coordinates are retained, then interpreted on the new common short-time axis. New pan defaults to center, Flutter depth to zero, ROOT to C3 with zero lane intervals, and both clock speed and delay scale to unity. States older than schema 13 retain LOG; new Motion amounts default to zero and all added resonance/spread values default to zero, with BAND, SINE and FADE selected. Older global sequencer limits are copied into every row; obsolete column locks and the removed global sequence lock are discarded. New effect lane locks default open, while existing sequence row and effect-pair locks survive. Older absolute-pitch development states first convert to a root and relative intervals. New modes default to SYNC. Older separate time controls migrate the active mode into the common position and range. Existing phaser speeds and random limits retain their Hz values when migrating from the former 12 Hz axis. The obsolete Output trim and mode-specific timing calibration are discarded; MIX defaults to 100% and comparison mode to A in older states; switching modes never changes the underlying delay position.

Presets and files

The shared preset bar writes .relay presets. Sessions include all parameters; sound presets exclude RUN and BYPASS. No external audio files are required.

Installation and help

Download the macOS archive from the product page and quit your audio host. Place the AU .component in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components, VST3 in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3, or CLAP in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP. The product page also offers an installer with format selection. Restart or rescan the host. To uninstall, quit the host and remove that product's bundles from those folders; saved sessions and user presets are separate.

For questions or a problem report, use BRK Audio support on GitHub. Include the plugin version, macOS version, host and format, sample rate and a short description of how to reproduce it.
