BRK - Hatch

User manual and parameter reference · 1.0.1

Build repeating hat, shaker and ride grooves from a seed, shape the sounds, and keep the take you like. Hatch plays its own synthesis engines and can send the rhythm as MIDI.

Quick startOperationEvery parameterPresets and filesInstallation and help

Quick start

  1. Insert Hatch on an instrument track, choose Trigger: Note, and hold a MIDI note or press PLAY.
  2. Choose A–D to hear the four starting grooves. Use NEW for another seed, MUTATE for a smaller change, and Closed/Open/Shaker/Ride to change the layer amounts.
  3. Select a sound tab to shape that layer, or select a step to edit it. Save your project or a user preset when you find a take to keep.

Editor window

The editor is 1000 by 740 pixels with HELP closed. Opening HELP adds a footer without moving the existing controls. The footer’s Manual / PDF action opens this guide. Shared cells, buttons and the preset bar support keyboard and VoiceOver interaction; drawing and dragging in the step grid require a mouse. Press HELP again to close the help area.

Operation

Note trigger plays while a note is held; Transport trigger follows the host transport. PLAY auditions without a note. The pattern follows the song position, so looping, scrubbing or offline rendering reproduces the same take.

MIDI notes 36–39, labelled C1–D#1 in Hatch, select slots A–D and open the note gate while held. Other notes play the selected slot. Note velocity changes level, and the modulation wheel raises closed-hat Density.

Each slot has its own seed, style, grid, layer amounts and variations. The slots share the cycle length, groove and sound definitions. A cycle can end between bar lines; it repeats at its own length and can drift across the host’s bars.

The four slot defaults are the starting grooves. NEW, MUTATE and DICE have a local history reached with the back/forward buttons. A grid paste cannot be undone; clipboard contents and the local take history are not saved with the project.

The five layers are closed hat, open hat, pedal, shaker and ride. Each has two selectable engines and its own sound settings. A closed or pedal hit chokes an open hat; the shaker and ride are independent.

MIDI Out sends General MIDI percussion notes on channel 10: closed hat 42, pedal 44, open hat 46, shaker 70 and ride 51. Host routing support differs by format; route Hatch’s MIDI output to another instrument in a host that supports it. This does not mute Hatch’s own audio.

HELP follows the hovered or keyboard-focused control. The parameter table documents host parameters; NEW, MUTATE, the dice buttons, PLAY, clipboard actions and hand-edited grid values are described in the operation sections.

Playing it

HELP opens a contextual footer for the hovered or keyboard-focused control; its Manual / PDF button opens the full reference at https://barak007.github.io/brk-audio/manuals/hatch/. Tab and VoiceOver reach named controls; drawing and dragging in the step grid use a mouse. Opening and closing help preserves the chosen width.

  • Trigger:
    • Note: the pattern plays while any note is held. Where it is in the pattern comes from the host’s song position, not from the note, so a note pressed mid-bar comes in mid-pattern and in time. A note up to 30 ms late still plays the hit it just missed, at once. With the transport stopped, the pattern runs from its first step at the note, at the host’s tempo (120 without one).
    • Transport: plays whenever the host plays.
  • PLAY (next to Trigger) plays the pattern without a note, the same way a held note does. Use it to audition, or in the standalone app.
  • The bars follow the host’s bar lines (its last bar start and bar number), also after a change of time signature.
  • C1 to D#1 pick slots A to D (each button shows its note), and also open the gate while held. The last slot picked, by a note or a click on A to D, stays picked after the note ends, and the buttons show it; any other note plays it.
  • Note velocity 100 and up plays the hits as they are; softer notes play them softer. The mod wheel takes the closed hat’s Density up toward every step.

The pattern

Four slots, A to D, each a pattern of its own. They start as House, Trap, Garage and Breaks. Every step of each layer gets a score: the style’s weight for that place in the beat, blended with the seed’s number for that step by Complexity. The steps are ranked by score and take hits from the top. Turning an amount up therefore only adds hits, and turning it down only takes the same ones away; a knob never reshuffles the groove.

  • Seed (0 to 1023): the take. NEW picks a random one.
  • Mutation: each step up re-rolls about an eighth of the steps, a different eighth each time. MUTATE takes one step.
  • DICE, in each section’s title (PATTERN, LAYERS, ROLLS, VARIATION, GROOVE, SOUND), rolls that section’s controls; DICE ALL rolls all of them at once, every sound too. Each control moves from where it is toward a random place in a span that stays musical (a closed hat’s decay 25 to 160 ms, Drive up to 9 dB, a ride’s engines a ride’s), as far as Range says: 10% nudges it, 100% lands anywhere in the span. A choice or a seed (Style, Shape, an engine, Decay Seed) changes with Range’s chance. A dice never moves the seed, Mutation, Grid, a sound’s Level or Pan, or the output. Range is kept with the session, not in presets.
  • < and > step back and forth through the slot’s NEW, MUTATE and DICE, each giving back exactly the controls it moved (kept while the plugin is open, the last 64).

PATTERN, per slot:

  • Style: Straight, House, Garage, Techno, Breaks, Trap, or Euclid. Euclid spreads the closed hits as evenly as they go, turned by the seed, so adding a hit spaces them out again.
  • Grid: 1/8, 1/8 T, 1/16 or 1/16 T steps.
  • Complexity: 0 plays the style as written; turned up, the seed pulls hits off the obvious places.
  • Evolve: how much each bar of the cycle differs from the others.
  • Accent: how much louder the strong places of the beat are than the weak ones.

LAYERS, per slot, in the grid’s order: how many hits each row plays.

  • Ride and Shaker: each up to every step (0 at first). The ride leans to quarters or off-beat eighths, the shaker to the sixteenths off the beat, by the style. Neither takes a step from the hats or cuts an open hat.
  • Open and Pedal: each up to half the steps. An open hat takes its step from the closed hat and the pedal.
  • Closed (its Density): the share of steps the closed hat plays.

ROLLS, per slot:

  • Rolls: how many closed hits become rolls. Rolls are likelier on the last beat, off the beat, and in the cycle’s last bar, and likelier still in Trap and Breaks.
  • Roll Min and Roll Max (2 to 16 hits): how many hits a roll splits its step into; the seed picks each roll’s count from Min to Max (the same for a fixed count). A step is the grid’s, so the count’s speed follows the grid: 2 on a 1/8 grid is a 1/16 roll, on a 1/16 grid a 1/32 roll; 3, 5, 6 and 7 give triplet and odd rolls. A roll fills only its own step, which swing shortens or lengthens.
  • Shape: how a roll’s level moves. Rising, Falling, Flat (all alike after an accented first) or Dip (softest in the middle); Mixed lets the seed pick for each roll. Depth: how far it moves, from every hit alike (0) to rising out of near silence (100%).
  • Speed: a roll’s timing. Faster bunches its hits toward the next step, a roll that speeds up; Slower bunches them at its start. A step’s own Timing stands in for it.

VARIATION, per slot, each with a seed of its own, so it can be picked again without touching the hits:

  • Decay Vary: how far each hit’s decay moves from its sound’s, up to an octave and a half shorter or longer, picked by Decay Seed (and moved by Evolve and Mutation like the hits), so a take rings the same every time.
  • Pan Vary: how far each hit moves from its sound’s Pan, either way (0: all in one place). Pan Drift: how, from each to a spot of its own (0) to all along one slow curve across the cycle (100). Both picked by Pan Seed. Panning only, so mono-safe.

Cycle (shared by the slots, left of the strip under the grid): how long before the pattern comes round again: whole bars, and eighth notes over them, up to 8 bars. It reads “4 bars”, “1 bar + 3/8” (a bar and three eighths) or “3/8”; type it the same way, or “1 3/8” or “1.375”. Whole bars follow the meter, and the eighths are eighth notes in any meter, so the cut lands on a step (inside one only on a 1/8 T grid). A cycle that ends mid-bar plays its last bar up to there and starts again, so it drifts across the bar lines and meets them again later (1 bar + 3/8 in 4/4, every 11 bars). The grid shades what is past the cut, and the strip draws that bar short.

GROOVE, shared by the slots:

  • Swing: 0 is straight. Turned up, the second step of each pair comes later: +67% is a triplet shuffle, +100% the hardest (three quarters through the pair). Turned down it comes early, a pushed, rushing feel (-100%: a quarter through). Swing On: Steps swings every second step of the grid; 1/8 swings the eighths, and a 1/16 grid’s steps move in proportion (on a 1/8 grid the two are the same). Triplet grids do not swing, nor does a pair the bar cuts short (the last of a 7/16 bar, say). The grid draws the steps where swing puts them.
  • Humanize plays each hit a touch late (up to 3% of a quarter note) and softer, the same amount each time it plays.

OUTPUT:

  • Dynamics: how much a hit’s velocity changes its level and brightness.
  • Low Cut: a high pass on everything, 12 dB an octave, from Off to 8 kHz, last in the chain before Output.
  • Output: the synth’s level; Off sends only MIDI.
  • MIDI Out sends the hits on channel 10 as General MIDI notes: closed F#1 (42), pedal G#1 (44), open A#1 (46), ride D#2 (51), shaker as maracas A#3 (70). An open hat’s note ends when the next closed hat or pedal starts, as its sound does; the ride’s lasts its decay. Letting go of the note ends every note.

The grid

One bar of the shown slot: one row each for ride, open, closed, pedal and shaker, each step as wide as swing makes it. A hit is as tall as it is loud, split where its roll’s hits fall, and each hit shows its ring: how long it rings at the tempo and how it fades (Hold), drawn up to the row’s next hit and cut where an open hat is choked. A roll’s count sits in its corner. The faint steps are the next ones Density would bring in. A dot marks a step set by hand.

  • Click a row’s name to switch that layer off (its row dims and reads MUTED; it plays no sound and sends no notes, so it chokes nothing either) or on again. Each layer’s switch is a parameter too (Closed On, Ride On…).

  • Click a step to select it; its sound shows in the SOUND tabs. The STEP row under the grid shows all its values: Velocity, Shape (its roll rising or falling), Hits, Timing (its roll slowing down or speeding up), Decay, Ring (tight or held), Pan and Sweep. Those the generator chose are dim, those set by hand bright. Drag one (or scroll it) to change it; double-click it to give it back. RESET gives back every value of the step. With no step selected, the row shows the step under the mouse (or the last one it was over), dimmer; pressing on it there selects it, and a drag goes on to change it.

  • Selecting holds the bar. The grid stops following the play: its bar number turns to the accent, a frame goes round it, and the ruler says HELD ON BAR 2. FOLLOW (or Esc) follows the play again; so does clicking the held bar in the strip. The arrow keys move the selection; Return adds or takes away the hit.

  • Double-click a step to add a hit or take it away.

  • Drag a hit to change two values at once, up and down one and sideways the other (each once it has moved 3 pixels), as the mode next to the strip says:

    Mode Up and down Sideways
    VELOCITY how loud (the numbers show it) a roll’s level rising (right) or falling
    ROLL how many hits, 1 to 16 a roll speeding up (right) or slowing down
    DECAY how long it rings, a quarter to four times its sound’s how it fades: tight (left) or held
    PAN a sweep across (up left to right), drawn as an arrow where it sits; it then stays put whatever Pan Vary does

    A sweep moves a roll’s hits along the arrow; a single hit glides along it as it rings (over the first half of its decay).

Each value set by hand stands in for the generator’s alone: a step given its own velocity still rolls as Rolls says. CLEAR gives back every hand-set step of the slot. Hand-set steps are saved with the session and presets, per slot, bar of the cycle and grid.

Copy, paste and clear are on right-click menus, on the thing itself:

  • a row: right-click it in the grid (its name or its steps) to copy it, every bar of it (all 8, also those past the cycle), to paste a copied row over it or add it, every bar, or to clear its edits. Any row onto any row: a shaker’s row can become the closed hat’s, in this slot or another.
  • a bar: right-click it in the strip under the grid to copy it, every row, to paste a copied bar over it or add it, or to clear its edits.
  • the whole cycle: right-click any bar in the strip, then Copy the whole cycle: the slot as it is, its settings and its hand-set steps. Pasted over another slot, that slot becomes the same, and stays live; added, only its hits (every row, every bar, as they play) come over and the slot keeps its own settings. Clear the cycle’s edits is CLEAR.

A row or a bar is taken as it plays: every step, the rests as well as the hits, with how loud, its roll (hits, shape and timing), its decay and ring, and a place or sweep set by hand. A bar the cycle cuts short is taken whole. Paste puts it in place of what is there, rests and all; Add lays only its hits there, so where it rests, what was there stays. The pasted steps become set by hand, so they play as they did wherever they land, and that slot’s knobs no longer move them (clearing gives them back to the generator). What stays the slot’s own: Pan Vary (each hit still moves about its sound’s Pan unless it was placed by hand), Humanize, and each hit’s small differences of sound. Since a pasted closed hit is set by hand, an open hat no longer takes its step. A row or a bar, or a cycle added, goes only onto a slot on the grid it was copied on (the menu’s item, greyed, says why). One thing is copied at a time, kept while the plugin is open, not saved; a paste cannot be undone.

The strip under the grid shows every bar of the cycle. Click a bar to hold it in the grid; click it again to follow the play; right-click it to copy, paste, add or clear. Faint over each bar, a line per layer whose hits ring for different lengths (Decay Vary, or set by hand): higher for longer, lower for shorter.

The sound

Synthesised live, so every sound control can be automated. Each hit’s small differences come from its seed, so a take plays back the same, sample for sample. The closed hat, the open hat, the pedal, the shaker and the ride each have a whole sound of their own, with the same controls. The SOUND tabs (CLOSED, OPEN, PEDAL, SHAKER, RIDE) pick which one shows; selecting a step in the grid shows its row’s. COPY TO copies the shown sound (Engine A to Attack, and Hold) to another, not its Decay, Level or Pan.

  • Engine A, Engine B and Morph, an equal-power crossfade between the two. Every sound can use every engine:
    • Metal: six detuned square waves (anti-aliased) into band and high passes. Color spreads them apart.
    • FM: a carrier and two inharmonic modulators, with feedback. Color sets how bright it is.
    • Modal: a noise burst into a bank of up to twelve plate-like resonators. Color sets how long the high modes ring.
    • Noise: filtered noise. Color sets how narrow and ringing it is.
    • Beads: seeded bead impacts into a small shell’s two resonances, a shaker. Color sets how many, from a rattle to a hiss; Pitch the shell’s size.
    • Bell: five inharmonic partials, the high ones dying first, a ride’s bell. Color stretches them from tuned to clangy.
    • Wash: twelve detuned high partials, each shimmering slowly, a ride’s body. Color sets how wide they spread and how much they shimmer.
  • Pitch (±12 semitones), Tone (high and low passes together, darker or brighter), Snap (a click of bright noise at the start of each hit), and Vary (each hit’s own tune, attack, decay and level).
  • Drive: tanh saturation with an anti-aliased first antiderivative, its input lifted so it keeps the hats’ top to about 16 kHz, faded in over its first 3 dB, with its level roughly matched.
  • Attack: how long each hit takes to come in, from struck (0.4 ms) to swished in (the shaker’s 11 ms) and on to 60 ms.
  • Decay: how long it rings, to -60 dB, times each hit’s own (Decay Vary, or set on the grid).
  • Hold: how the ring fades, ending at the same time. Tight drops fast and leaves a faint tail; Held stays up and then shuts; Natural (the middle) is the plain exponential fade. A step’s own Ring adds to it.
  • Level: how loud it sits against the others. Pan: where its hits sit; Pan Vary moves them about it.

The shaker starts as Beads into Noise, the ride as Wash into Bell (Morph between the ride’s wash and its bell). The ride rings on whatever the hats do. The pedal is the foot’s chick: a sound of its own (short, dark and quieter to start with), with a short mid knock of the plates meeting. Landing on a ringing open hat, it closes it as a foot does: the ring squeezes shut over 40 ms, its top going first. A closed hat (a stick) cuts it in 25 ms.

Pitch, Attack, the decays, Hold and the Modal, Bell and Wash engines’ tuning are set when a hit starts. Morph, Color (except those three’s), Tone, Drive, Low Cut and Output move hits that are still ringing too, glided over 20 ms. An open hat’s MIDI note lasts its decay (times its own), unless the next closed hat or pedal ends it first.

Presets and files

The host session saves the pattern, hand-edited steps, sound parameters and selected slot/preset. User presets also save the four slots, sounds and step overrides, with stable choice IDs.

User presets use .hatch files in ~/Library/Audio/Presets/Barakigal Audio/Hatch. There is no factory preset bank; the four initial slots are the starting points. Loading a preset leaves Bypass and Dice Range unchanged.

The copy/paste clipboard and the last 64 NEW/MUTATE/DICE history entries exist only while the plugin is open. A paste cannot be undone; save a preset or project before making a change you may want to keep separately.

Installation and help

Download the macOS archive from the product page and quit your audio host. Place the AU .component in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components, VST3 in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3, or CLAP in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP. The product page also offers an installer with format selection. Restart or rescan the host. To uninstall, quit the host and remove that product's bundles from those folders; saved sessions and user presets are separate.

For questions or a problem report, use BRK Audio support on GitHub. Include the plugin version, macOS version, host and format, sample rate and a short description of how to reproduce it.
