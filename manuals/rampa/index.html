User manual and parameter reference · 1.2.1

A tempo-locked generator for risers, transitions, downlifters and impacts.

Quick start

  1. Insert Rampa as an instrument and load a factory preset.
  2. Play a MIDI note or use Play; the Rise, Transition and Fall follow the song tempo.
  3. Adjust sources or modulation, then drag the rendered WAV cell into your DAW.

Editor window

1100 × 681 minimum; resizable. Dimensions are logical editor pixels, before host window chrome. HELP adds 92 pixels of height (Gear adds a one-pixel divider too). Allow enough usable screen space at your display scale. Press HELP again to close the help area.

Operation

The gesture is monophonic. Release mode decides whether note-off plays out, jumps to the fall, or releases the sound. The sources and effects have their own pages; turning a section off is separate from changing its parameters.

The Sample sound menu offers Load file for a new sample or relinking a missing one. Collect file into preset library copies an external sample into the Rampa user preset library. Copy that library, including Samples, when transferring collected patches. Uncollected files also retain a home-relative path where possible and an absolute fallback.

HELP follows the pointer or keyboard focus. Timeline and modulation drawing still use the pointer. The WAV render updates after edits; wait for a current render before dragging it out.

Modulator parameters are allocated in reusable slots. Unused slots remain listed in the host but have no audible role. Adding a modulator assigns the slot its controls; the Ramps and modulators section describes each type.

The gesture

  • Rise, Transition and Fall can each be switched on or off, so a gesture can be a Rise alone, a Rise into a Transition, a Fall alone, or all three. Their lengths are note values, from a 1/128 triplet to 32 bars (dotted and triplet values all the way down). A bar follows the host’s time signature.
  • The Transition says how everything gets from where the Rise left it to where the Fall starts:
    • Glide: moves along the Transition’s curve over its length.
    • Jump: switches at the drop, then stays for the Transition’s length.
    • Hold: stays where the Rise left it, then switches at the end.
    • Gap: silence for its length (the riser cutting out before the drop), then the Fall.
  • Time says where the Transition’s length comes from:
    • From rise (the default), From fall, Both: it is taken from the end of the Rise, the start of the Fall, or half from each. The section it takes from is squeezed: it still runs from its start level to its end level, only sooner. The gesture keeps its length and the Fall keeps its place on the grid.
    • Example: with a 4-bar Rise, a quarter-note Transition and a 4-bar Fall, From rise makes the Rise 3¾ bars and starts the Fall exactly on bar 5; Both centres the Transition on that bar line.
    • A section shorter than its share gives what it has, and the rest is the Transition’s own time.
    • Own: it is added between the Rise and the Fall, so the gesture gets longer by it.
  • ½ and ×2 (beside the tempo) halve or double the Rise, the Transition and the Fall at once, each to the nearest note value.
  • A note starts the gesture at once. While the host plays, the gesture then counts from the host’s song position, so it stays in time with the song. With the host stopped, it runs at the host’s tempo.
  • Release is what letting go of the key does:
    • Stop (the default): the sound fades out over 20 ms. The delay and reverb ring on.
    • Play out: nothing; the gesture plays to its end whatever the key does.
    • To fall: letting go during the Rise ends the Rise there, and the gesture goes on to the Transition and Fall. Hold a key to build; let go to drop.
  • More keys while one is held change only the note (legato). Each source glides to it over its own Glide. A key pressed after all keys were let go starts a new gesture. A gesture still sounding fades out over one cell (32 samples) first, so the restart does not click.
  • Seed picks the take of everything random (noise, Swarm, Sparks, the Voice’s breath, the Hit’s crack). Every gesture plays the same take.

Ramps and modulators

  • The Amp ramp is the level of everything over the gesture. It has four levels: at the start, at the drop, after the Transition and at the end. The Rise and the Fall each have a curve. Mirror (off by default) makes the Fall undo the Rise: it starts where the Rise got to and ends where the Rise started, whatever After and End say. The level is the ramp squared. Like a Ramp modulator it can also drive other controls (+ Target under its chip, or Amp in a control’s right-click menu), so a filter or a reverb send can move with exactly the shape of the level.

  • Curve shapes: every curve (each ramp’s Rise and Fall, and the Transition’s glide) has a shape as well as an amount. The amount is the diamond on the timeline; what it does follows the shape:

    Shape The amount For
    Bend how sharply it bends, either way (the only shape before) the surge before a drop
    Steep a Bend that goes four times as far: all but flat, then a shot to the other level at the very end (or start) extreme last-moment surges and snaps
    S-curve up: eases out and in; down: fast, slow, fast smooth swells and glides
    Steps 2 to 32 equal steps (2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32; 8 at rest) stair risers on the grid
    Accel 8 steps, each shorter than the last (up) or longer (down) the roll feel on any control
    Bounce up: lands and bounces off the end; down: bounces off the start; more is more bounces (1 to 16), and past 4 they stay tall and jiggle to the end impacts, Falls that bounce, jiggles
    Breath how deep it dips back just before the end, then lands the inhale before the drop
    Wobble how many swings around the straight path (1.5 to 12); up: they grow toward the end, down: they fade out tension that shakes before the drop
    Zigzag 2 to 16 saw teeth, each climbing and dropping back (at least 30 % of where it got, so it pumps right to the top); down: each jumps up and sags back pumping risers
    Elastic how many springs (1 to 8); up: shoots past the end and settles, down: winds up around the start, then springs snaps into the drop
    Jitter how wild a random wander around the straight path is; up: smooth, down: in steps nervous, unsteady movement

    Every shape starts exactly at its section’s first level and ends exactly at its last, so Mirror and To fall work with all of them. None but the Elastic leaves the range between the two (a Bounce never overshoots); an Elastic may, but never past 0 or 100 %. A Jitter’s take is the same every gesture: it follows the patch’s Seed, and each ramp and section has its own. Pick a shape with the small button in each section of the timeline, or with the ramp’s Rise shape and Fall shape cells.

  • Modulators are added from the + after the last chip (Ramp, LFO or Random), up to eight. Double-click a chip to give its modulator a name (up to 20 characters, saved with the patch; empty goes back to “LFO 2”), or use the small menu button on the selected chip to rename or remove it. A modulator drives any number of controls, each by its own depth. Add targets with + Target, or right-click any control and pick a modulator (or make a new one for it). A routing’s cell takes a drag for the depth, a double-click to type it and a right-click to invert or remove it. Modulators can drive other modulators’ controls, such as a Ramp speeding up an LFO.

    • Ramp: the same controls as the Amp ramp, 0 to 1 over the gesture.
    • LFO: Rate as a note value (8 bars to 1/128), Shape (sine, triangle, saw up, saw down, square), Phase, Polarity, Snap. Snap has three settings:
      • Off: the rate is used as set, so a modulated rate sweeps smoothly (and drifts off the beat while it moves).
      • On: the rate is rounded to a straight note value (1/4, 1/8, 1/16…), so a modulated rate steps up or down like gears; a new value takes over when the current cycle ends, so the wave never jumps and every cycle starts on a note.
      • Hz: the rate is in Hz (1/16 Hz to 64 Hz) and ignores the tempo. A knob position plays the speed its note value has at 120 BPM, so switching to Hz at 120 BPM keeps the speed.
    • Random: a new value every cycle, from a sequence that depends only on its Seed and the cycle, with Smooth gliding between values. Its Snap works as the LFO’s.
    • LFO and Random count their cycles from the start of the gesture.

  • A modulated control shows a track along its bottom: the reach of its routings, where it is set, and where it is now.

  • Between the modulators and the sources and effects, every routing to a control on a page is a wire down to that page’s tab, which gets a bar in the source’s colour. The selected source’s wires start at its routing cells and stand out; the others start under their chips, dimmer. Each wire brightens the more its routing moves its target right now. Click a routing (without dragging) to open its target’s page.

  • The timeline shows the sections on a bar grid, the Amp ramp filled, every modulator’s curve, where the Hit fires and the playhead. Behind the curves, faintly, is the gesture’s waveform as it sounds (its tails fainter), with its length in seconds at the bottom right. Rampa renders the patch again in the background a quarter of a second after you edit it (a control let go, a preset, the modulation, the sample, the tempo), while the editor is on screen. Host automation moving a control does not render it again: the waveform and the WAV are the patch as you set it. Nothing waits for it, and a soft shimmer runs across the waveform until the new one is ready (after a preset change the old waveform is hidden and the shimmer runs across the empty timeline). The time axis follows the section lengths only; tails that ring longer than the room after the end run off its right edge.

  • Drag WAV (beside Play) drags the gesture out as a WAV into the DAW or a folder: a held C4 at the host’s tempo, 24-bit stereo. It starts on the note’s first sample and runs to the first bar line after the gesture and its tails (until they fall under -80 dB), so its length is a whole number of bars to the sample. A tail that never dies (a frozen reverb) ends on the bar line after about 20 s more, faded over its last 10 ms. The file is kept in ~/Music/Rampa, since a DAW may play it from there, named with the patch, the tempo, the date and the time, and never written over another file. While the cell reads Rendering, a drag does nothing; it takes a moment after an edit. Click it to save it elsewhere. The selected ramp has handles:

    • Drag a square up or down for a level (Shift for fine steps).
    • Drag a diamond to bend a section’s curve (for shapes other than Bend and Steep, up raises the amount; the label says what it means, such as “16 steps”).
    • The small button at the top of each section (the Rise’s at its left, the Fall’s at its right, the Transition’s over it while it glides) shows the curve’s shape and opens a menu of the ten.
    • Double-click a handle for its default.
    • Click a curve to select its modulator.

Sources

Every source is transposed by the note (C4 plays untransposed) and has its own switch and level; a tab’s dot is filled while its source is on. All but the Hit have a Glide (how long they take to reach a new legato note; off by default). They sound alike at the same level.

  • Noise: white, pink or brown, decorrelated left and right by Width, or one of three that Texture shapes:

    • Crackle: clicks, 4 to 4000 a second, on both channels or (as often as Width says) on one.
    • Digital: the 1-bit shift-register noise of old game consoles, clocked at 1 to 128 times the note’s frequency, so it transposes with the note.
    • Metallic: six square waves at a TR-808 cymbal’s ratios, two octaves either way, transposed by the note; Width sets the right a hair sharper so they beat. A high-pass around 7 kHz makes it a hat.

    It has its own state-variable filter (low-, band- or high-pass at 12 or 24 dB/oct), whose cutoff can follow the note (Track). Spread pulls the left and right cutoffs apart (up to three octaves); Dual is two band-passes Spread apart in both channels: ramp the Spread and they part.

  • Tone: 1 to 9 voices (saw, square, triangle or sine) detuned by up to 200 cents and spread across Width. The saw and square are band-limited with PolyBLEP.

    • Shepard turns each voice into 2 to 8 octave copies under a fixed raised-cosine window, so Pitch rises or falls forever.
    • Crossing an octave hands each copy’s phase to the next, so the endless glide never clicks.
    • Chord puts the voices on a chord’s notes (octaves, fifths, major, minor, sus, major 7 or minor 7; its own, apart from the Swarm’s), a layer of octaves for each round of them: supersaw chord risers.

  • Swarm: 4 to 32 sines. Each starts at its own random pitch within Spread and drifts slowly. As Focus goes from 0 to 100 % they settle into the notes of the Chord (unison, octaves, fifths, major, minor, sus, major 7, minor 7) around the played note. Ramp Focus over the Rise and noise-like chaos lands in key at the drop.

  • Sub: a sine, triangle, Warm (a rounded square, harmonics a small speaker can play) or square, Pitch semitones from the note (two octaves down by default).

    • Clean (on by default) sends it around the Bus and the effects chain, joining with the Hit, so a high-pass, Comb, Crush or Shifter never thins it; it still gets the Width, Delay and Reverb.
    • During: Always, Before drop or After drop (it fades in or out over 5 ms at the drop).
    • Drop (down to -48 st) falls from the drop: most of the way in a third of Drop time, all of it by the Drop time. The 808 drop under an impact.

  • Sparks: short pings at random moments, Density per beat, each at a random pitch within Range around Pitch. Tonal pulls them onto the Chord’s notes.

  • Voice: a buzz at the note and breath noise through three formant filters.

    • Vowel morphs A, E, I, O, U; ramp it for a vowel that opens as it rises.
    • Formant shifts the formants (a smaller or larger singer), Breath mixes the buzz toward noise (a whisper), Sharp narrows the formants, Width moves them apart left and right.

  • Metal: two-operator FM. Ratio sets the modulator against the carrier (0.25 to 16; off the whole numbers it goes bell-like and metallic), Index its depth (the brightness: ramp it for a riser that opens up), Feedback the modulator on itself (toward noise), Width a slight detune between left and right. The index is held under the point where it would alias.

    • Stack adds the same pair an octave down, a fifth up and an octave up, all turned down together, so it grows bigger, not louder: a giant bell chord.
    • Ring adds four resonators on the Metal’s own strongest partials (its carrier and first sidebands, so they follow the Ratio). They take the Metal after the Amp ramp and keep ringing after it: louder and longer (0.4 to 6.4 s) as Ring goes up, through a Gap and past the gesture’s end. Release Stop fades them with everything else.

  • Hit: a one-shot at the drop (At: Drop, where the Rise ends) or where the Fall starts (At: Fall, for a Gap). The Amp ramp does not shape it: it has its own envelope.

    • A sine that starts Sweep semitones above Pitch and falls to it over Snap, dying over Decay.
    • Crack, a burst of high-passed noise, and Drive.
    • It joins after the effects that run in any order (so it is never filtered or gated shut) and before the Space and Reverb. It rings on past the gesture’s end; Release Stop fades it with everything else. A gesture fires it once, and the timeline marks where.

  • Sample: a sound read from memory, either one of five built in (Impact, Choir, Metal, Sweep, Glass, made in code so they are the same everywhere) or your own audio file. Pick it on the Sound cell (or Load file... there), or drop a WAV, AIFF or FLAC anywhere on Rampa. The patch keeps the file’s path: move the file and the Sound reads “(missing)” and stays silent. Up to 30 seconds are read. Every sound is taken as rooted at C4, so the note transposes it.

    • Grains mode: grains Size long (5 ms to 1 s), Density a beat (0.5 to 64), read around Position (0 to 100 % of the sound), scattered by Spray, played backwards as often as Reverse says (a backwards grain plays the same stretch, from its far end) and panned within Width. Like the other sources it follows the Amp ramp. Ramp Position across a Rise for a riser made of the sound.
    • One-shot mode: plays the sound once from Position, at the gesture’s start, the drop or where the Fall starts (At). With Reverse past half it plays backwards from the mirror point, and at the drop or the Fall it ends there instead of starting there, so it swells into it; when it is longer than the gesture before that point, it starts with the gesture, part way in, and still ends on it. Like the Hit, the Amp ramp does not shape it and it rings on past the gesture’s end; Release Stop fades it. The timeline marks its At point and, along the bottom, how long it plays.
    • The cells a mode ignores are dimmed (At in Grains; Size, Density, Spray and Width in One-shot), and in One-shot Reverse reads On or Off.

The Bus

Between the sources and the effects (the column between the two arrows), the Bus drives the sources, summed, into a limiter. It is off by default.

  • Drive (0 to 24 dB) pushes them in, and Ceiling (-24 to 0 dB) is where the limiter holds them: louder and denser, then squashed, so the effects get an even level however the sources stack up. Ramp the Drive and a riser gets denser instead of louder.
  • The gain falls at once with each peak and recovers over 60 ms. There is no lookahead, so it adds no latency.
  • The Hit joins after the chain, so it never goes through the Bus.

Effects

The chain: Filter, Drive, Crush, Gate, Phaser and Shifter in any order, then the Hit joins, then Space (Width and Delay), Reverb, Output and the Limiter. The effects tabs stand in the chain’s order, with arrows between them; drag one left or right to move its effect. The order is saved with the patch. Every effect works on the whole sound (all the sources but the Hit). A tab’s dot is filled while its effect is doing something and hollow while it is set to do nothing: Drive, Crush, Gate, Phaser, Shifter and the Delay start at 0 (their Amount, Mix or Depth), so they do nothing until that is raised.

  • Filter: low-pass, band-pass, high-pass, notch or peak at 12 or 24 dB/oct (trapezoidal state-variable, stable under fast modulation), with Resonance and key Track (the played note, not glided). Comb is a feedback comb tuned to Cutoff (with Track it plays the note); Resonance is its feedback, and Damp darkens each pass. Comb - feeds back upside down: only the odd harmonics, hollow and an octave lower. Morph crossfades low-pass (0) to band-pass (50 %) to high-pass (100 %) on the Morph control: ramp it and the filter turns inside out. Spread pulls the left and right cutoffs apart, up to two octaves.
  • Drive: Soft (tanh), Hard (clip), Fold (folds back from full scale) or Rectify (both halves turned up: the octave above), at 0 to 36 dB with Mix. Bias moves the signal off the curve’s centre so its halves bend apart (even harmonics, a tube’s grit); the DC it leaves is blocked. Tone is a low-pass after the curve (off at 20 kHz). The output is turned down by about a third of the drive in dB, so more drive makes the sound denser more than louder. It runs at four times the sample rate. It fades in over its first 3 dB, so 0 dB is clean.
  • Crush: a lower sample rate (Rate, held samples, aliasing on purpose) and fewer bits (Bits, 1 to 16, between whole numbers too), with Mix. Off at Mix 0.
    • Follow (on by default) counts the bits from the sound’s own level, so a quiet start crunches as hard as a loud end; off, they count from full scale and a quiet sound rounds away to silence.
    • Track moves the Rate with the note, so the aliasing plays it; Jitter stretches each held sample by up to an octave either way (a wobbling clock); Tone is a low-pass on what comes out.
  • Gate: the roll that speeds up toward the drop. Rate is a note value, with Depth, Duty and Smooth for the edges (at least 0.6 ms, so it never clicks). Snap keeps it on straight note values locked to the gesture’s grid; off, its rate sweeps freely.
    • Swing lengthens the first of each pair of cycles and shortens the second, up to a triplet shuffle.
    • Pattern turns it into a step gate: each step lasts one cycle of Rate, Length (1 to 16) says how many steps loop, and the row of steps says which open. Steps past the Length are dimmed.
  • Phaser: Phaser (4, 8, 12 or 16 all-pass Stages), Flanger (a 0.3 to 10 ms delay) or Through-zero (a flanger whose delay sweeps through the dry’s own, upside down, so they cancel where they meet: the jet that closes to nothing; the dry is 3 ms late). An LFO at Rate (a note value, Shape sine, triangle, up or down) sweeps it by Depth around Centre; ramp the Centre for the jet sweep. Feedback (either sign), Spread (the right channel’s LFO up to half a cycle behind) and Mix: 100 % is dry and wet half and half, where the notches are deepest. Off at Mix 0.
  • Shifter: a frequency shifter (every frequency moves by the same number of hertz, up to 5 kHz either way, fine near 0) in a loop through a short Delay: with Feedback each pass shifts further, the endless barber-pole rise or fall. Off at Mix 0.
    • Track scales the shift with the note (at 100 % an octave up shifts twice as far), so a patch keeps its colour across the keyboard.
    • Both ways shifts the right channel the other way: up on the left, down on the right.
    • Low cut and High cut in the loop keep the passes from piling up as rumble or fizz.
    • Sync sets the Delay as a note value (Note, a 1/128 triplet to a bar), so each pass lands on the grid.
    • Spread turns the right channel’s carrier up to 90 degrees, which widens it without ever cancelling in mono.
  • Space:
    • Width (mid/side, 0 to 200 %).
    • A tempo-synced delay (a 1/128 triplet to a bar, ping-pong or stereo, damped feedback up to 95 %).
    • In its loop, Low cut thins each repeat more than the last and Saturate makes high feedback bloom instead of piling up.
    • Mode: Tape glides to a new time (the repeats bend in pitch); Jump crossfades to it over 30 ms.
  • Reverb: an eight-line feedback delay network with a pre-delay (Pre-delay, up to 250 ms), input diffusion, damping and a decay time (RT60) of 0.2 to 20 s. Size and Decay can be modulated.
    • Mod drifts the lines’ lengths slowly (read with a cubic between samples), so long tails on sines do not ring metallic.
    • Freeze holds the tail: no more input and no loss. Drive it with a Ramp (a Jump Transition, or a Steps shape) to freeze the wash at the drop and let it go later.
    • Shimmer feeds the tank’s output back in pitched by Interval (+12, +7, +19 or -12 semitones), bounded so it can never run away, and kept between 250 Hz and 9 kHz so it never builds a low tone under a long tail.
  • Limiter (Limit in the header, on by default): a stereo-linked lookahead limiter holding peaks under -0.3 dBFS. Its 1.3 ms lookahead is there even when it is off, so the latency the host sees never changes.

The delay and reverb run after the gesture ends, so tails ring past the last note.

Factory presets

The presets are grouped by job, each group a submenu of the preset list. Each preset shows one or two ideas, and its modulators are named for what they do: load one, select a modulator’s chip to see what it drives, and change the one thing suggested.

Presets and sessions store every choice by a stable ID beside its position (<PARAM id="delayTime" value="15" choice="1/8d"/>, and p2="sine" on a modulator for its choices), and read the ID first, so a list that gains entries never turns an older file’s setting into another. Choices are not open to host automation (a host stores a choice as its position in the list); the on/off switches are toggles hosts may automate.

Presets and files

Presets are browsed with the arrows or the preset-name menu. A star marks an edited patch. Save supports folders, Rename, Delete and opening the user preset folder; dropping a compatible preset file onto the bar loads it. A newer-file indication means this version could not apply every setting.

The host session stores the complete patch. A product preset can deliberately leave listening trim, audition or display controls unchanged; the product-specific rules below describe those exceptions.

Rampa presets use .rampa in ~/Library/Audio/Presets/Barakigal Audio/Rampa. Output stays unchanged on preset loading. User samples are referenced rather than embedded in a preset file; transfer the collected Samples folder as well as the .rampa files. A missing source is shown as missing and remains silent until relinked with Load file.

Installation and help

Download the macOS archive from the product page and quit your audio host. Place the AU .component in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components, VST3 in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3, or CLAP in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP. The product page also offers an installer with format selection. Restart or rescan the host. To uninstall, quit the host and remove that product's bundles from those folders; saved sessions and user presets are separate.

For questions or a problem report, use BRK Audio support on GitHub. Include the plugin version, macOS version, host and format, sample rate and a short description of how to reproduce it.
