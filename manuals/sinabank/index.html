BRK - Sinabank

User manual and parameter reference · 0.1.0

A polyphonic additive synth whose one oscillator is a bank of up to 1024 sine partials per voice. Two frames, a filter, a drawn mask and four drawn envelopes shape every partial before the bank is summed.

Quick start

  1. Insert Sinabank on an instrument track and play MIDI notes, or click the keyboard at the bottom of the window.
  2. Open the preset menu and load a factory sound from Keys, Bass, Leads, Pads, Plucks, Bells or Textures, or begin from Init. Each factory sound sets up Macro 1 and Macro 2 for quick changes.
  3. Pick Frame A and Frame B and turn Morph, then shape the partials with Tilt, Even and Stretch and the filter's Cutoff. Draw a frame of your own in the FRAME A or FRAME B tab, or a mask in the MASK tab.

Editor window

The editor opens at 1200 by 745 pixels and can be resized up to 2400 by 1600; made taller, Env 1 to Env 3 grow to fill the left column. The top bar holds the voice controls, the right side the SPECTRUM, MASK, FRAME A and FRAME B tabs over the mod matrix and the amp envelope. Cells and the preset bar support keyboard and VoiceOver interaction; the frame, mask and envelope drawings require a mouse.

Operation

Each cell is dragged or scrolled, with Shift for finer steps; double-click to type a value and Alt-click for its default. Envelope slot and mod matrix amounts work the same way; click a slot's name for its target or source.

The spectrum view shows every sounding voice's partials, the newest brightest, with the filter and mask curve over them. When no note plays it previews a C3. The START corner draws one cycle of the wave a note starts with.

UNDO and REDO on the tabs' row, or Cmd-Z and Cmd-Shift-Z, step through the drawings: frames, envelopes and the mask. The host's own undo covers the knobs.

Mod Rate in the top bar sets how often each voice works out its partials' gains: every 4 samples by default, at every sample (exact), or every 16 or 32 samples to save CPU, with the gains gliding in between. A voice whose gains have nothing moving them skips the work.

MIDI events land on their own sample. The sustain pedal holds notes. Pitch bends glide sample by sample.

Partials

  • Partials: how many sound, 1 to 1024, and it can move while notes play (the last one fades in or out). None go past the Nyquist frequency: they fade out between 42% and 49% of the sample rate and are not worked on above it, so a bend never aliases and a high note costs less. At 48 kHz all 1024 fit only under about 23 Hz; a C3 fits about 180.
  • Tilt: tilts the frames, in dB for each octave of partial number.
  • Even: the even partials’ level; at 0 only the odd ones are left.
  • Stretch: partial k sits at k^(1 + stretch / 1000%) times the fundamental, from -1000% (every partial on the fundamental, which sums loud) to +1000% (partial k at k squared).
  • Phase: where each note starts the wave, 0 to 360 degrees (at 90 a sine starts on its peak).
  • Random: scatters each partial’s starting phase at random on each note, either side of Phase (at 100% up to half a cycle each way): the same spectrum, a smoother wave.
  • Spread: the partials alternately left and right, on top of the frames’ pans. The odd partials alternate among themselves (3 left, 5 right, 7 left…) and so do the even ones (2 left, 4 right…), so with Even down the odd ones are still spread evenly. The fundamental stays in the middle and the mono sum is untouched.
  • Damp: each partial fades over the note, faster the higher it is (at 100%, 40 dB a second for each octave of its number: partial 2 loses 40 dB a second, partial 8 120), as a string or a bell loses its highs first. A note that starts its envelopes again starts its damping again too.
  • Damp Curve: bends the damping over the partials’ octaves. Above 0 the low and middle partials fade nearly as fast as the highs; below 0 only the very highest fade quickly. At 0 it is the straight line above.
  • Strum Start, Strum End and Strum Curve: bring each note’s partials in one after another, the fundamental at Start (up to 2 s after the note) and the highest partial at End, the ones between spread over the partials’ octaves on the curve: above 0 the middle partials come in near End, below 0 near Start. Start past End brings the highs in first. Each partial comes in over 3 ms. A mono note that starts its envelopes again strums again, and a strum its envelopes start once the note sounds fades the late partials out; either way they fall back out no faster than they came in.
  • Shift Start, Shift End and Shift Curve: move every partial but the fundamental by a number of Hz (up to 2 kHz either way), so the spectrum is no longer harmonic while the pitch stays (a frequency shifter’s sound): the second partial by Start, the highest by End, the ones between over the partials’ octaves on the curve (above 0 the middle partials move nearly as far as End, below 0 they stay near Start). A partial pushed under 4 Hz falls silent.
  • Octave and Fine.

The spectrum view’s corner (START) draws one cycle of the wave the shown note starts with: its partials at their start phases, Random left out.

Morph

Two frames, A and B. Each one gives every partial a level, a pitch offset (a percentage of its own pitch), a pan and a phase, and the morph glides each partial’s level, pitch, pan and phase between them. A phase moves while the note plays without nudging the partial’s pitch (the output takes each phasor at an angle), and phases glide as plain numbers, so a frame that turns its partials several times over unwinds smoothly. Morph sets where between them a note sits (0 is A, 100% is B); an envelope slot sent to Morph moves it from there (by default Env 2 carries each note from A to B over a second and back over 50 ms when it is released). Whatever the mix, the partials are scaled to the same loudness. Each frame is one of:

  • Saw, Square, Triangle (with its partials’ phases, so it is a triangle wave), Pulse (25%), Sine: the classic waves.
  • Organ (drawbars), Octaves (only the partials an octave apart), Formant (two humps), Comb (every 6th partial notched), Noise (random levels).
  • Bell (partial k at k^1.3), Piano (a stiff string’s sharpening), Metal (random offsets up to 30%), Detune (random offsets up to 1%): these move the partials’ pitches.
  • Split (the partials alternately left and right, as Spread does) and Scatter (random pans): these pan them.
  • Disperse (each partial late by the square of its number, as a chain of allpass filters smears a saw), Cosine (every partial a quarter turn on: a peaky wave), Flip (the even partials turned over) and Scramble (random phases): saws with their phases moved. Morphing a saw into one of them changes its wave and attack, not its spectrum.
  • Custom: the frame’s own drawing (below).

The FRAME A and FRAME B tabs over the spectrum draw a frame’s partials as bars, one lane at a time: Level (in dB), Offset (in semitones, on a cubic scale so a small detune gets room, ±48), Pan (left at the top) and Phase. The zoom at the right shows the first 16, 64, 256 or all 1024 partials. Drag across the bars to draw; double-click a bar to put it back (a saw’s level, no offset, pan or phase). Drawing on a frame that plays one of the fixed shapes copies that shape into the frame’s drawing and switches the frame to Custom, so a preset is a starting point. The drawings are saved with the project.

UNDO and REDO on the tabs’ row (or Cmd-Z and Cmd-Shift-Z) step back and forward through the drawings: each stroke or double-click on a frame, an envelope or the mask is one step, with the frame’s choice when the drawing made it Custom, so a drawing replaced by drawing over a fixed shape comes back. The host’s own undo covers the knobs.

Filter

Low pass, High pass or Band pass, with Cutoff, Resonance (in dB), Slope (6 to 96 dB/oct, past a soft knee) and Key (how far the cutoff follows the note). Res Shape is Peak, Narrow or Wide (one bump), Twin (two, half an octave either side), Notch (a dip) or Ripple (peaks every half octave), and Res Pos moves it up to four octaves from the cutoff.

Mask

A drawn gain curve over the spectrum, on top of the filter, in the MASK tab over the spectrum: its middle line changes nothing, its top boosts by 24 dB and its bottom cuts by 96 dB, so it can cut as deep as a filter. Amount scales both sides together (at 50% the top is +12 dB and the bottom -48 dB); the lines across the drawing show the dB at the current amount. Draw it as an envelope (drag points, bend the lines by their middle dots, double-click to add or remove a point). The tab’s header turns it on or off and picks what it runs over:

  • Hz: 20 Hz to 20 kHz on an octave scale. The mask stays put across the keyboard, as a formant or a body does.
  • Partials: partials 1 to 1024 on an octave scale. It follows the partials’ numbers, so it moves with the note.

Shift slides the drawing up to 8 octaves either way (past its ends, the end values carry on). Key (in Hz) makes it follow the note: at 100% it moves an octave with each octave played, as Partials does, with C4 where it is drawn. Mask Shift and Mask Amt are slot targets too, so an envelope can sweep a drawn formant. The spectrum view’s curve is the filter and the mask together.

SHAPES in the header picks a built-in drawing, which sets the mode it runs in and turns the mask on: Flat, the vowels A, E, I, O and U (three formants each), Telephone (a 400 Hz to 3 kHz band), Air (a lift over 5 kHz), Body (three low resonances), Scoop (a dip around 800 Hz), Notch (a deep cut at 1 kHz), Dark and Bright (tilts) over Hz; No Fundamental, First Eight, Hump (a peak around the 8th partial) and Fade Highs over the partials. A shape is a drawing like any other once picked, and UNDO takes it back.

Envelopes

Four drawn envelopes: Amp (under the spectrum), which always sets the level, and Env 1, Env 2 and Env 3, which can also loop. Each is points from start to end, a curve on each segment, and a sustain point (the white one, with the dashed line). A note plays the shape up to the sustain point, holds there while the key is down, and plays the rest once it is released. Time is how long the whole shape lasts, and Velocity how much a soft note scales the envelope down (the amp’s level, and how far any envelope moves its targets).

  • Drag a point (the start and end only up and down; the amp envelope’s end stays at zero, so every note ends silent).
  • Drag a segment’s middle dot to bend it; double-click it to straighten it.
  • Double-click empty space to add a point, or a point to remove it.
  • Move the sustain point by dragging the tab on top of its dashed line; it snaps to the nearest point.

A note released before its sustain point goes on from there, easing from its level onto the shape over that segment. A mono note that starts the envelopes again fades from where they are to the shape’s start over 3 ms, then plays the whole shape again. A new note starts right on the shape, so a shape that starts above zero gives an attack as sharp as drawn.

Mod

Each envelope has four slots: click a slot’s name for its target, drag its number for how far the envelope moves that target at its top (Shift for fine steps, Alt-click for none). Two slots on the same target add up. Every voice follows its own envelopes. The targets, grouped in the menu, and how far a slot’s full turn reaches:

  • Pitch (48 semitones) and Level (all of it: at -100% it silences the note at the envelope’s top).
  • Partials: Partials (1024), Tilt (24 dB an octave), Even, Stretch (1000%), Phase (a whole cycle), Phase Rnd (Random’s 100%), Spread, Damp, Damp Curve (its whole range), Strum Start and Strum End (2 s), Strum Curve, Shift (2 kHz on Start and End together), Shift Start and Shift End (2 kHz) and Shift Curve. Phase and Phase Rnd are read as a note starts.
  • Morph (all of it).
  • Filter: Cutoff (10 octaves), Res (30 dB), Res Pos (4 octaves) and Slope (90 dB an octave).
  • Mask: Mask Shift (8 octaves) and Mask Amt (all of it).
  • Envelopes: Amp Time, Env 1 Time, Env 2 Time and Env 3 Time (4 octaves of time: +100% makes it 16 times as long, -25% half as long), each voice’s own.
  • Macros: Macro 1 to Macro 4 (±100%), each voice’s own: the macro’s knob plus every other source’s move, kept between 0 and 100%, is what that macro sends to its own targets. A macro does not move the macros.

Pitch, Partials, Stretch, Strum and Shift turn on a cubic scale, so small moves get most of the travel. The filter’s and the mask’s Key, Fine and Octave are not targets: the Key source on Cutoff or Mask Shift, or a slot on Pitch, does the same.

By default Env 1 opens the cutoff (330 Hz) by five octaves and falls back over the first fifth of its time, a pluck, and Env 2 morphs from A to B.

Each envelope’s header has a Free / Sync switch. Free, its Time is in milliseconds; Sync, the same cell picks a note length from 4 bars to 1/32, dotted and triplet, at the host’s tempo (1 bar to start). Its Time targets scale either. Env 1, Env 2 and Env 3 also have a Mode (Envelope, or Loop: the drawing repeats from each note’s start as an LFO, with no sustain hold) and a Polarity (Unipolar, the bottom being no move, or Bipolar, moving both ways around the drawing’s middle line).

Voice

In the top bar.

  • Mode: Poly plays up to 16 notes; a stolen voice fades out over 2 ms. Mono plays one, starting the envelopes again on each note. Legato plays one and starts them again only when no key was held. Both mono modes go back to the newest key still down when the one playing is let go.
  • Glide: how long each note takes to slide from the last one’s pitch, the same time however far it goes. In Poly each new note slides from the newest voice’s pitch.
  • Bend: the pitch wheel’s range.
  • MPE: on (the default) or off (see MPE).
  • MPE Bend: an MPE note’s own wheel’s range (see MPE).

The sustain pedal holds notes.

Bypass is the host’s own bypass (an instrument has no Bypass button of its own): the sound fades out over 15 ms, then every voice stops and notes are ignored until it is switched off. The session keeps it.

Mod matrix

Eight slots under the spectrum, each a source, a target and an amount: click the slot’s top line on the left for its source and on the right for its target, and drag its number for how far the source moves the target at its full level (Shift for fine steps, Alt-click for none). The targets and their ranges are the envelope slots’ (above). A bar along each slot’s bottom shows the newest note’s source now. Over the slots are four Macro knobs, sources the host can automate. The sources, grouped in the menu:

  • Amp, Env 1, Env 2, Env 3: the envelopes, as their own slots read them, so an envelope can reach more than four targets.
  • Velocity: the note’s, 0 to 1.
  • Key: the note, 0 at C4 and ±1 four octaves away.
  • Random: a new value from 0 to 1 on each note (and on each new note of a mono voice), so each note can sound a little different.
  • Pressure: with MPE, each note’s own channel pressure; otherwise the channel’s aftertouch, or a key’s polyphonic aftertouch on that key (whichever is higher).
  • Slide: CC 74, the MPE timbre axis; with MPE each note’s own channel’s, otherwise CC 74 on any channel.
  • Mod Wheel: CC 1.
  • Bend: the pitch wheel bending the note, -1 to 1 (the master wheel, plus with MPE its own channel’s).
  • Breath: CC 2, and Expression: CC 11, on any channel.
  • Macro 1 to Macro 4: the knobs over the slots, moved by any slot that targets them (an envelope sweeping a macro sweeps everything the macro reaches), each voice on its own.

The controllers settle over about 3 ms, the macros over about 15.

MPE

With MPE on (top bar), notes can come on a zone’s member channels (2 to 16, or as an MPE Configuration Message sets the lower and upper zones), each following its own channel’s pitch wheel, pressure and slide. MPE Bend is how far a member channel’s wheel bends its note (48 semitones; the host can set it with RPN 0, which holds until MPE Bend is moved). A bend or pressure sent just before a note-on applies from the note’s first sample, and each note’s bend settles over about 3 ms. The master channel’s wheel bends every note over Bend. MPE is on by default: a plain keyboard on channel 1 plays exactly as without it, but one sending on another channel bends by MPE Bend. With MPE off every channel plays as plain MIDI.

Presets and files

The preset bar steps through the factory presets in seven groups (Keys, Bass, Leads, Pads, Plucks, Bells, Textures), then the user's, with Init, Save as, Rename and Delete. A file dropped on the bar loads.

User presets are .sinabank files in ~/Library/Audio/Presets/Barakigal Audio/Sinabank; a name typed with a slash, such as Dub/Tape Echo, makes a folder. A preset holds every parameter but Output, Mod Rate and Bypass, plus the drawn envelopes, mask and frames; loading one clears the drawings' undo.

The host session recalls every parameter and drawing. Every choice is stored by a stable ID beside its position, so older presets and projects keep playing the same entries. No samples or external files are needed.

Installation and help

Download the macOS archive from the product page and quit your audio host. Place the AU .component in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components, VST3 in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3, or CLAP in ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/CLAP. The product page also offers an installer with format selection. Restart or rescan the host. To uninstall, quit the host and remove that product's bundles from those folders; saved sessions and user presets are separate.

For questions or a problem report, use BRK Audio support on GitHub. Include the plugin version, macOS version, host and format, sample rate and a short description of how to reproduce it.
