Gear Exciter

Find the weight and sparkle your track is missing.

Give a thin sound more body, or bring a dull part a little closer. Shape the low-end weight and high-end detail, add some colour, and blend it in until the part sits where you want it.

A brushed-metal panel with separate controls for tone, harmonics and the final blend.

What this does

Downloads the release archive from GitHub, checks it against the checksum fixed at release time and copies the chosen formats into ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins. Installs in your own plugin folders without sudo, refreshes the Audio Unit list when AU is selected, and replaces each installed format only once its new bundle is fully copied.

To read the script first, download it and open it in TextEdit (this runs nothing):

curl -fsSL https://github.com/barak007/brk-audio/releases/download/gear-exciter-v0.1.1/install.sh -o install.sh && open -e install.sh

Version 0.1.1 / macOS universal / VST3, AU, CLAP / Free early access

  • Add low-end body with Contour and open up the highs with Process
  • Adjust Align to explore how the bass, mids and highs sit together
  • Bring out extra colour and detail with Harmonics
  • Use Focus and Colour to choose where that detail sits and how bright it feels
  • Try Double Trouble when you want a stronger lift
  • Blend the effect with Mix, and arm Auto Gain before a change to help match the listening level
  • Start from a preset and use the needle meter to keep an eye on the level

Changelog

0.1.12026-11-11Latest
  • Remove the help panel’s X button; press HELP again to close it.
  • Seal AU, VST3 and CLAP bundles with complete ad-hoc signatures during packaging.
  • Shared numeric cells now apply typed values only with Enter; Escape, focus loss and outside clicks cancel entry.
0.1.02026-11-10
  • Auto Gain arms idle, learns only after direct sound-knob edits, and stays idle after preset/session loads. Presets exclude Auto Gain; offline rendering holds the saved Output.
  • Power and host bypass retain the processing latency and fade without freezing the processing history. Both quality settings share a fixed latency and the harmonic path fades around switching.
  • Rendered controls support named keyboard/VoiceOver values, and HELP links to the HTML/PDF manual.
  • Double Trouble in the top bar doubles Process and Contour's boosts in dB and the added harmonic signal, with a smooth transition. It saves with presets and sessions, defaults off and keeps Align's timing. Like every switch, it does not start Auto Gain; only direct user sound-knob edits do.
  • A taller front gives every knob's scale and label their own space: Process above Contour and Align, Harmonics above Focus and Colour, and Mix above Output in one column.
  • First version: the bands (Process, Contour, Align) and the harmonic stage (Harmonics, Focus, Colour) on a plain editor; drawn as a piece of gear: a brushed-silver front with eight knobs on printed scales, a VU on the output level, Link and Quality toggles and a power switch.
  • The harmonics are stronger and hold their proportion on quiet material (they used to fall away under about -30 dBFS): at 100% they add about -9 dB against the 6-12 kHz band of noise, and Harmonics now starts at 30%, not 0.
  • The knobs are grouped on the front: Align, Process and Contour under TIME ALIGNMENT, Harmonics, Focus and Colour under HARMONIC STAGE, and Mix and Output together under BOTH STAGES, since Mix blends both with the dry signal.
  • Auto Gain, in the top bar: keeps the processed signal as loud as the dry input by turning the Output knob (-12 to +12 dB), working for eight seconds after a direct sound-knob edit. Double Trouble does not start learning. A HELP bar says what the control under the mouse is and what it does.
  • Twelve factory presets in three groups: Clarity (time alignment), Harmonics (the harmonic stage) and Utility.
