BRK - Sinabank

Build a sound from its partials up.

Make keys, bells, basses and slowly turning pads out of hundreds of sine waves. Pick or draw two spectra and morph between them as a note plays, then let drawn envelopes reshape every partial at once. Factory sounds in seven groups to start from.

Download for macOS

Or install from Terminal

curl -fsSL https://github.com/barak007/brk-audio/releases/download/sinabank-v0.1.0/install.sh | bash
What this does

Downloads the release archive from GitHub, checks it against the checksum fixed at release time and copies the chosen formats into ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins. Installs in your own plugin folders without sudo, refreshes the Audio Unit list when AU is selected, and replaces each installed format only once its new bundle is fully copied.

To read the script first, download it and open it in TextEdit (this runs nothing):

curl -fsSL https://github.com/barak007/brk-audio/releases/download/sinabank-v0.1.0/install.sh -o install.sh && open -e install.sh

Changelog

0.1.02026-11-25Latest
  • A long SysEx message from the host no longer allocates memory on the audio thread; it was ignored before and still is.
  • Add Bypass, the host's own bypass (no button: an instrument is switched off in the host): the sound fades out and notes are ignored until it is off.
  • Double-click envelope/matrix destination amounts to type in the displayed units (octaves, dB, percentages, time factors, etc.); Return on the focused amount also opens typing.
  • VoiceOver names and reads every cell (as a slider, a list or a switch) and the preset bar, and presses them; Tab reaches them, the arrow keys move a focused cell as the wheel does (Shift finer, Up the next entry of a list) and Return does what a click does. Each modulation slot reads as its source and target (lists that open their menus) and its amount (a slider the keys move); the envelopes' and the mask's header words, UNDO, REDO and SHAPES are pressed as a click presses them, and the SPECTRUM, MASK and FRAME tabs can be picked, so every view is reached. The mouse works as before; the frame, mask and envelope drawings are still mouse-only.
  • First version: a 16-voice additive synth whose one oscillator is a bank of up to 1024 sine partials, shaped by Tilt, Even and Stretch (-1000% to +1000%).
  • Two frames, A and B, each a level, pitch offset, pan and phase for every partial, picked from twenty shapes (Saw, Square, Bell, Organ, Formant, Split, Disperse, Cosine and more) or drawn partial by partial in the FRAME A and FRAME B tabs, and a Morph between them that glides all four.
  • The partial count can be moved up and down while notes play.
  • A filter that is a gain curve over the partials (low, high or band pass, any slope from 6 to 96 dB/oct), with six resonance shapes that can sit up to four octaves either side of the cutoff, and key tracking.
  • Four drawn envelopes (Amp, Env 1, Env 2, Env 3): points, a curve on each segment and a sustain point, with one Time for the whole shape and a Velocity amount each; beside the sustain point, the time up to it and the release's.
  • Four mod slots on each envelope, sending it to any of 33 targets, grouped in the menu: Pitch and Level; every Partials control from Tilt to Shift Curve (Phase and Phase Random as a note starts); Morph; Cutoff, Res, Res Pos and Slope; Mask Shift and Mask Amt; each envelope's Time; and the four Macros, so an envelope moving a macro moves everything the macro reaches; each voice on its own.
  • Env 1, Env 2 and Env 3 can loop as LFOs, unipolar or bipolar, and every envelope has a Free / Sync switch: synced, its Time cell picks a note length at the host's tempo, and its Time target still scales it.
  • Phase and Random (a scatter either side of Phase) set where each note starts its partials, and a START view draws the wave a note starts with.
  • Damp: each partial fades over the note, the higher ones faster, with a Damp Curve over the partials.
  • Strum: each note's partials come in one after another, the fundamental at Strum Start and the highest at Strum End (either way round), with a Strum Curve between.
  • Shift: every partial but the fundamental moved by a number of Hz, from Shift Start on the second partial to Shift End on the highest, with a Shift Curve between.
  • Undo and redo for the drawn frames, envelopes and mask (Cmd-Z and Cmd-Shift-Z too); the host is told the project changed when a drawing does.
  • A plucky default patch: 256 partials, the cutoff at 330 Hz with no resonance, a 0.5 s amp envelope and Env 1 falling over its first fifth.
  • A drawn Mask over the spectrum in its own tab, in Hz or by partial number, from +24 dB to -96 dB scaled by Amount, with Shift up to 8 octaves, Key to follow the note in Hz, an on/off switch, and 17 built-in shapes (vowels, telephone, air, body, notch, tilts and more); Amount and Shift are slot targets.
  • A mono-safe Spread that alternates the odd partials among themselves and the even ones among themselves, so it stays even when Even is turned down.
  • Poly, Mono and Legato modes, Glide, and the pitch wheel's range.
  • A mod matrix of eight slots, each a source (Amp, Env 1-3, Velocity, Key, Random per note, Pressure, Slide, the Mod Wheel, Bend, Breath, Expression or one of four Macro knobs the host can automate), a target and an amount, with the source's level shown live.
  • MPE, on by default (and a switch to turn it off, so a keyboard on any channel plays as plain MIDI): per-note pitch bend (MPE Bend, 48 st, or as the host's RPN 0 sets until MPE Bend is moved), pressure and slide (CC 74) on member channels, with zones from MPE Configuration Messages; without MPE, Pressure and Slide follow channel or polyphonic aftertouch and CC 74.
  • Pitch bends glide sample by sample, and every MIDI event lands on its own sample.
  • A Mod Rate setting: the partials' gains are worked out every 4 samples by default, or at every sample (exact), or every 16 or 32 samples to save CPU; a voice whose gains have nothing moving them skips the work.
  • Output starts at -3 dB.
  • Presets: 56 factory presets, eight in each of seven groups (Keys, Bass, Leads, Pads, Plucks, Bells, Textures), each with its macros set up, and the user's own, saved with the drawn envelopes, mask and frames, in folders of their own.
  • Presets and sessions store every choice by a stable ID as well as its position, so adding frames, targets or other entries later never changes what an older preset or project plays.
  • Choices (frames, filter type, targets, sources, note lengths, modes) are no longer open to host automation, which would follow their place in the list; the on/off switches (Mask, MPE, Sync) and every knob still are, and a click flips MPE.
  • The window opens at its smallest size (1200 x 745); made taller, Env 1 to Env 3 grow to fill the left column.
  • A live spectrum of every voice, the newest brightest; when they stop, the last note stays shown as it ended (a C3 preview before any note has played).
