BRK - Rampa

Build the tension. Make the drop your own.

Give the next section an entrance. Layer tones, noise and effects into a rise, a transition or an impact that follows your song's tempo, then play it from your keyboard or drag it into the arrangement as audio.

Download for macOS

Or install from Terminal

curl -fsSL https://github.com/barak007/brk-audio/releases/download/rampa-v1.2.1/install.sh | bash
What this does

Downloads the release archive from GitHub, checks it against the checksum fixed at release time and copies the chosen formats into ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins. Installs in your own plugin folders without sudo, refreshes the Audio Unit list when AU is selected, and replaces each installed format only once its new bundle is fully copied.

To read the script first, download it and open it in TextEdit (this runs nothing):

curl -fsSL https://github.com/barak007/brk-audio/releases/download/rampa-v1.2.1/install.sh -o install.sh && open -e install.sh

Changelog

1.2.12026-11-11Latest
  • Remove the help panel’s X button; press HELP again to close it.
  • Keep hovered control help visible across timer refreshes; unchanged keyboard focus no longer replaces it.
  • Seal AU, VST3 and CLAP bundles with complete ad-hoc signatures during packaging.
  • Rename the plugin to BRK - Rampa in hosts and installed bundles; keep plugin IDs, saved host sessions and the existing preset folder.
  • Unify the header: 16 px title, presets beside it, right-aligned version/build details, shared neutral colours and square controls.
  • Apply typed numeric values with Enter; Escape, focus loss and clicks outside cancel entry.
1.2.02026-11-10
  • Added toggleable contextual HELP and a website/PDF parameter manual.
  • The Sample menu can collect files into the preset library; presets and sessions keep library/home-relative sample references with absolute paths as fallback.
  • VoiceOver names and reads every cell (as a slider, a list or a switch), the preset bar, ½ and ×2, and the Rise, Transition and Fall shape buttons by name; Tab reaches them, the arrow keys move a focused cell as the wheel does (Shift finer) and Return does what a click does. The mouse works as before; the timeline and the modulator views are still mouse-only.
  • Cells drag evenly when Shift is pressed or let go mid-drag, scroll as far as a trackpad moves instead of racing, and record a whole scroll as one undo step. Their text is a little smaller and a cooler white, the same as the other plugins on the shared cell look.
1.1.02026-11-05
  • Choices are saved by stable IDs beside their positions (in presets, sessions and the factory presets), so presets and sessions keep their settings when a list gains or reorders entries. The modulators' choices (Mirror, Shape, Polarity, Snap, the curve shapes) and routings from the Amp are saved by ID and name too. Files from 1.0.0 load as before.
  • Choices (lengths, modes, shapes, waves, the Sample's sound and the like) can no longer be automated by hosts, which drops any automation already recorded on them. The on/off switches were already toggles and stay automatable.
1.0.02026-11-04
  • LFO and Random Snap has a third setting, Hz: the rate in Hz (1/16 Hz to 64 Hz), free of the tempo.
  • A snapped LFO or Random whose rate is modulated changes speed at the end of a cycle, instead of jumping mid-wave.
  • The selected modulator's controls sit under a line in its colour, so they read as that chip's.
  • First version: a mono generator of risers, downlifters and transitions. A note starts a gesture of up to three sections (Rise, Transition, Fall), each switchable, with lengths in note values (a 1/128 triplet to 32 bars) locked to the host's tempo.
  • The Transition is set to Glide, Jump, Hold or Gap.
  • The Transition's time is taken from the end of the Rise by default, so the Fall stays on the grid; it can also come from the Fall, from both, or be its own.
  • Letting go of the key stops the sound (Stop, the default), does nothing (Play out) or ends the Rise (To fall).
  • The Amp ramp shapes the level, and can drive other controls with the same shape. Up to eight modulators (Ramp, LFO, Random) can each drive any number of controls, including other modulators. Add targets from the modulator or by right-clicking a control. Modulators can be renamed (double-click their chip).
  • Every curve has a shape: Bend, Steep (an extreme Bend), S-curve, Steps, Accel, Bounce (up to 16 bounces), Breath, Wobble, Zigzag, Elastic or Jitter, picked with a small button in each section of the timeline.
  • Nine sources, all transposed by the note:
    • Noise (white, pink, brown, or Crackle, Digital and Metallic, which a Texture shapes; a 12 or 24 dB filter with a left/right Spread, or two band-passes Spread apart)
    • Tone (a stack detuned by up to 200 cents, on a chord of its own if you like, or an endless Shepard tone)
    • Swarm (sines that settle from random pitches into a chord)
    • Sub (four waves; Clean keeps it around the effects; plays always, before or after the drop; a pitch Drop from the drop)
    • Sparks
    • Voice (a buzz and breath through formants, with a Vowel to morph)
    • Metal (two-operator FM; Stack adds pairs an octave down and a fifth and an octave up, Ring adds resonators that ring on after it)
    • Hit (a one-shot at the drop or where the Fall starts, with its own envelope)
    • Sample (five built-in sounds or your own audio file, kept by its path; in grains, or once at the start, the drop or the Fall; reversed, a one-shot swells into the drop or the Fall and ends on it, and the timeline marks where it plays) Each but the Hit has its own Glide for legato notes.
  • Effects: Filter (with Notch, Peak, a Comb tuned to the note (upside down too, and damped), a Morph from low- to band- to high-pass, and a stereo Spread), Drive (four times oversampled; Soft, Hard, Fold or Rectify, with Bias and Tone), Crush (sample rate and bits, counted from the sound's own level; Track, Jitter, Tone), a Gate for rolls that speed up (with Swing and a 16-step pattern), Phaser (4 to 16 stages), Flanger or Through-zero, with four LFO shapes, and a Shifter (a frequency shifter in a feedback loop, for barber-pole rises: up to 5 kHz, with Track, Both ways, a Low and High cut in the loop and a synced delay). They run in any order: drag their tabs. The sources stand apart from the effects, with arrows showing the way the sound goes. A tab's dot shows whether its effect is doing anything (most start at a Mix of 0).
  • A Bus between the sources and the effects drives the sources into a limiter (Drive, Ceiling), off by default.
  • Then Width, a synced Delay (with a Low cut and Saturate in its loop, and a Jump mode that crossfades to a new time) and a Reverb (with Pre-delay, slowly moving lines, Freeze and Shimmer).
  • A lookahead limiter at the end holds peaks under -0.3 dBFS (Limit, on by default).
  • Drag the gesture out as a WAV (Drag WAV, beside Play), whole bars long to the sample with its tails, and see its waveform under the timeline on the same grid; both come from a rendering made in the background after you edit the patch (host automation does not render it again). Dragged files are named with the date and never overwrite one already there.
  • ½ and ×2 halve or double every section's length at once.
  • A hundred and two factory presets, grouped by job: Risers, Downlifters, Transitions & Drops, Impacts, Rhythmic, Textures, Complex (many moving parts), Micro (a beat or less), Extremes (settings pushed to the edge) and Drum FX (one-shots for a kit: kick, hats, snares, clap, tom, rim, crash, reverse cymbal). Each shows one idea, and its modulators are named for what they do. The README lists what each one shows and what to try.
